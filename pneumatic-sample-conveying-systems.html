What a sample conveying system does

A sample conveying system is a closed tube network between the place a sample is taken and the place it is tested. An operator loads the sample into a carrier at a sending station. Plant air moves the carrier through the tube. A receiving station in the laboratory opens the carrier so the chemist can run the sample immediately.

  • Carriers travel at more than 100 km/h.
  • Routes are laid out for melt shops, casters, pouring points, kilns, and furnaces.
  • The line can return results to the operating area within seconds.
  • Each sample conveying system is drawn for that plant’s distances, bends, and stations.

Manual sample walking adds minutes to every heat. A pneumatic line removes that wait, which is why installation cost is often recovered within a year through higher throughput and less idle time.

Sample coin conveying systems

A sample coin conveying system is the version of this line built for coin samples: the disc-shaped metal specimens prepared for a spectrometer. Steel plants also call these discs sample coins. The carrier has to hold the coin securely, keep it from contamination, and arrive at the laboratory receiving station ready for analysis.

Kerri sizes the tube, carrier, and stations for those sample coins, and for other sample shapes a plant already uses. Sending stations sit at the melt shop or caster. The receiving station sits in the central laboratory, next to the spectrometer and, where needed, beside a macro etching unit.
